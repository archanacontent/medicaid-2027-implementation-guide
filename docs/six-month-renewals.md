# Six-Month Renewals

## The Renewal Rules

<span class="mcd-label">Automatic renewal first</span>
States must try to renew coverage using data they already hold. When the data isn't enough, they send a prepopulated renewal form and give the member at least 30 days to respond.

<span class="mcd-label">Two ways to make the switch</span>
CMS's [State Medicaid Director Letter of March 6, 2026](https://shvs.org/resources/new-cms-guidance-on-six-month-renewals-in-medicaid/) lets states keep each member's existing renewal date and start six-month periods at the next renewal, or bring renewals forward from January 2027. Bringing them forward can't start before January 1, 2027, can't create a period shorter than six months, and requires a notice to every member whose 12-month period is cut short.

<span class="mcd-label">Mixed households</span>
Members of one household can now have different renewal cycles. When a six-month renewal turns up information that affects another member, the state must treat it as a change in circumstances.

## Where The Two Deadlines Collide

States that bring renewals forward risk a [renewal surge in January 2027](https://ccf.georgetown.edu/2026/03/06/cms-releases-guidance-on-6-month-medicaid-renewals-for-expansion-adults/), the same month work checks begin. Caseworkers would then face two new processes at once, members would get more notices at once, and CMS has warned that processing backlogs could put a state at risk of compliance action. The safer path, keeping existing renewal dates, spreads that load across the year.
